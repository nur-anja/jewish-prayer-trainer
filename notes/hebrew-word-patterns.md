# Hebrew word patterns (before binyanim)

Most Hebrew words are built from a small set of prefixes, suffixes and templates. Learning them lets you look at a word and see how it's built from its root, without studying binyanim first.

## How to take a word apart

1. **Strip the one-letter prefixes** at the front (and, the, in, to, from…).
2. **Strip the suffixes** at the end (our, your, plural…).
3. **Check for a template letter** that is part of the word's form, like a tense prefix or a noun-forming mem or tav.
4. **What remains is usually the 3-letter root.** If you only find two letters, the missing one is usually nun, yod, vav or he. These "weak" letters tend to drop out.

## 1. Prefix letters that stick to the front

The mnemonic is "Moshe ve-Khalev" (Moses and Caleb), which spells out most of them.

| Prefix | Meaning | Example | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ו | and | ואהבת | ve-ahavta | and you shall love |
| ב | in, with | בכל | be-khol | with all |
| ל | to, for | לעולם | le-olam | forever (to the world) |
| כ | like, as | כמוך | kamokha | like you |
| מ | from | מעולם | me-olam | from eternity |
| ה | the | הגדול | ha-gadol | the great |
| ש | that, who | שעשה | she-asah | who made |

They can stack, as in this example ("and in all"):

| Example | Transliteration | Breakdown |
|---|---|---|
| ובכל | u-ve-khol | and + in + all |

## 2. Possessive endings (my, your, our…)

These are everywhere in prayers.

| Suffix | Meaning | Example | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| י | my | צורי | tzuri | my rock |
| ך | your (singular) | לבבך | levavkha | your heart |
| ו | his | שמו | shmo | his name |
| נו | our | אלהינו | Eloheinu | our God |
| כם | your (plural) | אלהיכם | Eloheikhem | your God |
| ם | their | אבותם | avotam | their fathers |

## 3. Plural and "of" forms

| Ending | Meaning | Example | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ים | masculine plural | מתים | metim | the dead |
| ות | feminine plural | מצות | mitzvot | commandments |
| י | "of" (plural construct) | אלהי | Elohei | God of |
| ת | "of" (feminine construct) | מלכות | malkhut | kingship (also an abstract-noun ending) |

## 4. Future tense: four prefix letters

Yod is one of four. The mnemonic is the word "eitan" (aleph, yod, tav, nun).

| Prefix | Who | Example | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| א | I | אברך | avarekh | I will bless |
| י | he, they | ימלך | yimlokh | he will reign |
| ת | you, she | תשמע | tishma | you will hear |
| נ | we | נברך | nevarekh | we will bless |

In prayers, the future often reads as a wish ("may it…"):

| Example | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| יתגדל | yitgadal | may it be magnified (Kaddish) |

## 5. Past tense: endings instead of prefixes

| Ending | Who | Example | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (none) | he | בחר | bachar | he chose |
| תי | I | אהבתי | ahavti | I loved |
| ת | you | בחרת | bacharta | you chose |
| נו | we | חטאנו | chatanu | we sinned |
| ו | they | שמרו | shamru | they kept |

Watch out for the "nu" ending. It can mean "we did" in the past, or "us" as an object:

| Example | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| קדשנו | kideshanu | he sanctified us |
| וצונו | ve-tzivanu | and commanded us |

## 6. Present tense, the "-er/-ing" form

There are two common shapes. The first is a vowel "o" after the first root letter, often written with a vav:

| Example | Root | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| שומר | שמר | shomer | guards, guardian |
| גואל | גאל | go'el | redeems, redeemer |
| זוכר | זכר | zokher | remembers |

The second is a mem prefix:

| Example | Root | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| מחיה | חיה | mechayeh | revives |
| מלביש | לבש | malbish | clothes |
| מקדש | קדש | mekadesh | sanctifies |

## 7. Nouns made with mem

A mem prefix often means "the place, thing or result of" the root:

| Noun | Root | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| מקום | קום | makom | place (where one stands) |
| מצוה | צוה | mitzvah | commandment |
| משפט | שפט | mishpat | judgment |
| מקדש | קדש | mikdash | sanctuary |
| מעשה | עשה | ma'aseh | deed |

## 8. Nouns made with tav

| Noun | Root | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| תפלה | פלל | tefilah | prayer |
| תהלה | הלל | tehilah | praise |
| תורה | ירה | torah | teaching |
| תשובה | שוב | teshuvah | return, repentance |

## 9. Abstract nouns: the "-ah" pattern

The shape is root letters, then an "u" vowel before the last one, then a he at the end. It often names a quality or an act.

| Noun | Root | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| גאולה | גאל | ge'ulah | redemption |
| גבורה | גבר | gevurah | might |
| ישועה | ישע | yeshu'ah | salvation |
| קדושה | קדש | kedushah | holiness |
| ברכה | ברך | berakhah | blessing |

## A full peel, step by step

| Step | Hebrew | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Word | ובמצותיו | u-ve-mitzvotav, "and in his commandments" |
| Strip "and" + "in" | מצותיו | |
| Strip "his" | מצות | |
| Strip plural | מצו | |
| Strip noun mem | צו | two letters left, so a weak letter dropped out |
| Root | צוה | command |

Once these feel familiar, binyanim come easily. They are just more templates, like the mem and tav patterns above.
