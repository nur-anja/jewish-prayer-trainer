# This and that

Hebrew has its own words for "this" and "these". For "that" and "those" it uses the words for "he", "she" and "they".

## This and these

"This" and "these" are words of their own:

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| זֶה | this (masc.) |
| זֹאת | this (fem.) |
| אֵלֶּה | these |

## That and those

"That" and "those" are the words for "he", "she" and "they" with "the" in front:

| Example | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| <b style="color:red">הַ</b>הוּא | he | that (masc.) |
| <b style="color:red">הַ</b>הִיא | she | that (fem.) |
| <b style="color:red">הָ</b>הֵם | they (masc.) | those (masc.) |
| <b style="color:red">הָ</b>הֵן | they (fem.) | those (fem.) |

The vowel under "the" changes with the next letter, but the letter stays the same.

## After the noun

A word for "this" or "that" comes after its noun, like an adjective, and takes "the" when the noun has it:

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| הַדְּבָרִים <b style="color:red">הָ</b>אֵלֶּה | the words the these (these words) |
| בַּיָּמִים <b style="color:red">הָ</b>הֵם | in the days the those (in those days) |

## Watch out

Without "the", these words mean something else:

| Example | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| הֵם | no "the": the word for "they" | they |
| זֶה אֵלִי | no "the", before the noun: a sentence | this is my God |
