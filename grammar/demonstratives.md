# This and that

Hebrew has its own words for "this" and "these". For "that" and "those" it uses the words for "he", "she" and "they".

## This and these

"This" and "these" are words of their own:

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| זֶה | this (masc.) |
| זֹאת | this (fem.) |
| אֵלֶּה | these |

## That and those

"That" and "those" are the words for "he", "she" and "they" with "the" in front:

| Example | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| <b style="color:red">הַ</b>הוּא | the word for "he" | that (masc.) |
| <b style="color:red">הַ</b>הִיא | the word for "she" | that (fem.) |
| <b style="color:red">הָ</b>הֵם | the word for "they" (masc.) | those (masc.) |
| <b style="color:red">הָ</b>הֵן | the word for "they" (fem.) | those (fem.) |

On its own, the word after "the" means "he", "she" or "they"; with "the" in front, it always means "that" or "those". The vowel under "the" changes with the next letter, but the letter stays the same (see [prefix letters](prefix-letters.md)).

## After the noun

A word for "this" or "that" comes after its noun, like an adjective, and takes "the" when the noun has it:

| Example | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| הַדְּבָרִים <b style="color:red">הָ</b>אֵלֶּה | after the noun, which also has "the" | these words |
| בַּיָּמִים <b style="color:red">הָ</b>הֵם | after the noun, whose "the" hides in the vowel of "in" | in those days |

English puts "this" and "that" before the noun and doesn't add "the"; Hebrew puts them after the noun and repeats its "the". On how "the" hides after "in", "to" and "like", see [prefix letters](prefix-letters.md#the-hiding-inside-in-to-like).

## Watch out

Without "the", these words mean something else:

| Example | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| הֵם | no "the": the word for "they" | they |
| זֶה אֵלִי | no "the", before the noun: a sentence, with "is" understood | this is my God |
