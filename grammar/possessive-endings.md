# Possessive endings

These are everywhere in prayers. Instead of a separate word for "my", "your" and so on, Hebrew adds an ending to the noun:

| Ending | Meaning | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| ־י | my | צוּרִ<b style="color:red">י</b> | my rock |
| ־ךָ | your (singular) | לְבָבְ<b style="color:red">ךָ</b> | your heart |
| ־וֹ | his | שְׁמ<b style="color:red">וֹ</b> | his name |
| ־נוּ | our | אֱלֹהֵי<b style="color:red">נוּ</b> | our God |
| ־כֶם | your (plural) | אֱלֹהֵי<b style="color:red">כֶם</b> | your God |
| ־ם | their | אֲבוֹתָ<b style="color:red">ם</b> | their fathers |

The vowel before the ending also changes from word to word, but the ending's letters stay the same.

## Same endings on prepositions

The same endings attach to small words like "to" and "in", and to verbs as their object (see [object endings](object-endings.md)):

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| לָ<b style="color:red">נוּ</b> | to us |
| בּ<b style="color:red">וֹ</b> | in him, in it |

## Watch out

The ending for "our" also appears on past-tense verbs, where it can mean "we did" or "us". See [past tense](past-tense.md).
