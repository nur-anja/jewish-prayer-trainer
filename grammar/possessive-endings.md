# Possessive endings

Instead of a separate word for "my", "your" and so on, Hebrew adds an ending to the noun. These are everywhere in prayers.

| Suffix | Meaning | Example | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| י | my | צורי | tzuri | my rock |
| ך | your (singular) | לבבך | levavkha | your heart |
| ו | his | שמו | shmo | his name |
| נו | our | אלהינו | Eloheinu | our God |
| כם | your (plural) | אלהיכם | Eloheikhem | your God |
| ם | their | אבותם | avotam | their fathers |

## Same endings on prepositions

The same endings attach to small words like "to" and "with":

| Example | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| לנו | lanu | to us |
| בו | bo | in him, in it |

## Watch out

The "nu" ending also appears on past-tense verbs, where it can mean "we did" or "us". See [past tense](past-tense.md).
