# Possessive endings

Instead of a separate word for "my", "your" and so on, Hebrew adds an ending to the noun. These are everywhere in prayers.

| Suffix | Meaning | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| ־ִי | my | צוּרִי | my rock |
| ־ְךָ | your (singular) | לְבָבְךָ | your heart |
| ־וֹ | his | שְׁמוֹ | his name |
| ־ֵנוּ | our | אֱלֹהֵינוּ | our God |
| ־כֶם | your (plural) | אֱלֹהֵיכֶם | your God |
| ־ָם | their | אֲבוֹתָם | their fathers |

## Same endings on prepositions

The same endings attach to small words like "to" and "in":

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| לָנוּ | to us |
| בּוֹ | in him, in it |

## Watch out

The "nu" ending also appears on past-tense verbs, where it can mean "we did" or "us". See [past tense](past-tense.md).
