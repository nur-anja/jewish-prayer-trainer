# Letter assimilation (hidamut)

A נ with no vowel of its own, right before another letter, usually merges into that letter: the נ drops out, and the next letter gets a dagesh to show it is now doubled:

| Spelled out | Written | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| וַתִּנְתֵּן | וַתִּתֵּן | and she gave |

The spelled-out form is never written; it only shows where the נ was. Said quickly, the נ sounds like the letter after it, so the two became one doubled letter, and the dagesh is the trace of the missing נ. This merging is called hidamut, assimilation.

## Roots starting with נ

In the [future tense](future-tense.md), the prefix comes right before the first root letter, so a first נ merges into the second root letter:

| Example | Root | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| וַתִּתֵּן | נתן | dagesh in the second ת replaces the נ | and she gave |
| אֶשָּׂא | נשא | dagesh in the ש replaces the נ | I will raise |

So when a future verb shows only two root letters and the first of them has a dagesh, try נ as the missing first letter.

## מ for "from"

The [prefix](prefix-letters.md) מ for "from" is short for the word מִן, and its נ merges into the next letter the same way:

| Prefix | Meaning | Example | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| מִ־ | from | <b style="color:red">מִ</b>מֶּרְחָק | dagesh in the מ replaces the נ | from afar (from distance) |
| מִ־ | from | <b style="color:red">מִ</b>כָּל | dagesh in the כ replaces the נ | from all of |

Before א, ה, ח, ע and ר, which can't take a dagesh, the prefix becomes מֵ־ instead: the longer vowel makes up for the missing doubling.

## Watch out

A dagesh doesn't always mean a missing נ. In the same word, one dagesh can come from something else:

| Example | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| וַתִּתֵּן | dagesh in the first ת: from the "and" that flips the tense; dagesh in the second ת: the missing נ | and she gave |

The "and" that flips the tense is in [future tense](future-tense.md#and-that-flips-to-the-past). Put back a נ only if it gives a real root.
