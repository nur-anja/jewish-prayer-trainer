# Letter assimilation (hidamut)

A נ with no vowel of its own, right before another letter, usually merges into that letter: the נ drops out, and the next letter gets a dagesh to show it is now doubled:

| Spelled out | Written | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| וַתִּנְתֵּן | וַתִּתֵּן | and she gave |

The spelled-out form is never written; it only shows where the נ was. Said quickly, the נ sounds like the letter after it, so the two became one doubled letter, and the dagesh is the trace of the missing נ. This merging is called hidamut, assimilation.

## Roots starting with נ

In the [future tense](future-tense.md), the prefix comes right before the first root letter, so a first נ merges into the second root letter:

| Example | Root | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| וַתִּתֵּן | נתן | dagesh in the second ת replaces the נ | and she gave |
| אֶשָּׂא | נשא | dagesh in the ש replaces the נ | I will raise |

So when a future verb shows only two root letters and the first of them has a dagesh, try נ as the missing first letter. It is the usual answer, but not the only one: a few roots with another first letter do the same, the most common being [the root לקח](#the-root-לקח).

## מ for "from"

The מ for "from" in [prefix letters](prefix-letters.md) is short for the word מִן, and its נ merges into the next letter the same way:

| Spelled out | Written | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| <b style="color:red">מִן</b> מֶרְחָק | <b style="color:red">מִ</b>מֶּרְחָק | from distance (from afar) |
| <b style="color:red">מִן</b> כָּל | <b style="color:red">מִ</b>כָּל | from all of |

The dagesh in the letter after the מ replaces the נ. Before א, ה, ח, ע and ר, which can't take a dagesh, the prefix becomes מֵ־ instead: the longer vowel makes up for the missing doubling.

## The root לקח

The root for "take" starts with ל, but in the future tense its ל merges into the next letter:

| Example | Root | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| וַתִּקָּחֵהוּ | לקח | dagesh in the ק replaces the ל | and she acquired it |
| יִקַּח | לקח | dagesh in the ק replaces the ל | he will take |

It is the only root where ל does this; other roots starting with ל keep it.

## Watch out

A dagesh in the same word can come from something other than a missing נ:

| Example | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| וַתִּתֵּן | dagesh in the first ת: from the "and" that flips the tense; dagesh in the second ת: the missing נ | and she gave |

The "and" that flips the tense is in [future tense](future-tense.md#and-that-flips-to-the-past). Put back a נ only if it gives a real root.
