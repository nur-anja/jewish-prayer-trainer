# Prefix letters

Some short words are written as a single letter attached to the front of the next word. The mnemonic "Moses and Caleb" spells out most of them:

| Mnemonic | Translation |
|---|---|
| מֹשֶׁה וְכָלֵב | Moses and Caleb |

Each prefix letter stands for a short word:

| Prefix | Meaning | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| וְ־ | and | <b style="color:red">וְ</b>אָהַבְתָּ | and you shall love |
| בְּ־ | in, with | <b style="color:red">בְּ</b>כָל | with all |
| לְ־ | to, for | <b style="color:red">לְ</b>עוֹלָם | forever (to the world) |
| כְּ־ | like, as | <b style="color:red">כְּ</b>צֵל | like a shadow |
| מִ־ | from | <b style="color:red">מִ</b>מִּצְרַיִם | from Egypt |
| הַ־ | the | <b style="color:red">הַ</b>גָּדוֹל | the great |
| שֶׁ־ | that, who | <b style="color:red">שֶׁ</b>עָשָׂה | who made |

The vowel under the prefix can shift depending on the next letter, but the letter stays the same.

## Stacking

Prefixes can stack, and "and" always comes first:

| Example | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| <b style="color:red">וּבְ</b>כָל | and + in + all | and with all |

## "The" hiding inside in, to, like

After the "in", "to" and "like" prefixes, the ה of "the" drops out and only its vowel stays:

| Example | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| <b style="color:red">בְּ</b>שָׁמַיִם | shva: no "the" | in heaven |
| <b style="color:red">בַּ</b>שָּׁמַיִם | patach: "the" is hidden in the vowel | in the heavens |

So the vowel under the prefix tells you "the" is there, usually a patach.

## Watch out

A word starting with one of these letters doesn't always have a prefix, because the letter may be part of the root:

| Example | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| מֶלֶךְ | the מ is part of the root | king |

Strip a letter only if what's left is a real word.

A kamatz under a prefix doesn't always hide "the": before a hataf vowel, the prefix takes the matching short vowel:

| Example | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| <b style="color:red">כָּ</b>אֳנִיּוֹת | kamatz only because of the hataf kamatz after it: no "the" | like ships of |
