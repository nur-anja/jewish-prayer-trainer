# Past tense

In the past tense, an ending (not a prefix) shows who did the action. The bare root form means "he did".

| Ending | Who | Example | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (none) | he | בחר | bachar | he chose |
| ה | she | אמרה | amrah | she said |
| תי | I | אהבתי | ahavti | I loved |
| ת | you | בחרת | bacharta | you chose |
| נו | we | חטאנו | chatanu | we sinned |
| תם | you (plural) | שמעתם | shma'atem | you heard |
| ו | they | שמרו | shamru | they kept |

## Watch out: "nu"

The "nu" ending can mean "we did" in the past, or "us" as an object:

| Example | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| חטאנו | chatanu | we sinned |
| קדשנו | kideshanu | he sanctified us |
| וצונו | ve-tzivanu | and commanded us |

The blessing formula has both object forms:

| Example | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| אשר קדשנו במצותיו וצונו | asher kideshanu be-mitzvotav ve-tzivanu | who sanctified us with his commandments and commanded us |

## "And" that flips to the future

In biblical text, a vav before a past form can turn it into future or command:

| Example | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| ואהבת | ve-ahavta | and you shall love |
