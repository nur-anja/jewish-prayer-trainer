# Past tense

In the past tense, an ending (not a prefix) shows who did the action. The bare root form means "he did".

| Ending | Who | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| (none) | he | בָּחַר | he chose |
| ־ָה | she | אָמְרָה | she said |
| ־תִּי | I | אָהַבְתִּי | I loved |
| ־תָּ | you | בָּחַרְתָּ | you chose |
| ־נוּ | we | חָטָאנוּ | we sinned |
| ־תֶּם | you (plural) | שְׁמַעְתֶּם | you heard |
| ־וּ | they | שָׁמְרוּ | they kept |

## Watch out: "nu"

The "nu" ending can mean "we did" in the past, or "us" as an object:

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| חָטָאנוּ | we sinned |
| קִדְּשָׁנוּ | he sanctified us |
| וְצִוָּנוּ | and commanded us |

The blessing formula has both object forms:

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו וְצִוָּנוּ | who sanctified us with his commandments and commanded us |

## "And" that flips to the future

In biblical text, a vav before a past form can turn it into future or command:

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| וְאָהַבְתָּ | and you shall love |
