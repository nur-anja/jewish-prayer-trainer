# Past tense

In the past tense, an ending (not a prefix) shows who did the action, and the bare form with no ending means "he did":

| Ending | Who | Example | Root | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (none) | he | בָּחַר | בחר | he chose |
| ־ה | she | אָמְרָ<b style="color:red">ה</b> | אמר | she said |
| ־תִּי | I | אָהַבְ<b style="color:red">תִּי</b> | אהב | I loved |
| ־תָּ | you | בָּחַרְ<b style="color:red">תָּ</b> | בחר | you chose |
| ־נוּ | we | חָטָא<b style="color:red">נוּ</b> | חטא | we sinned |
| ־תֶּם | you (plural) | שְׁמַעְ<b style="color:red">תֶּם</b> | שמע | you heard |
| ־וּ | they | שָׁמְר<b style="color:red">וּ</b> | שמר | they kept |

## "And" that flips to the future

In biblical text, a ו before a past form can turn it into future or command:

| Example | Root | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| <b style="color:red">וְ</b>אָהַבְתָּ | אהב | and you shall love |

The opposite flip, from future to past, is in [future tense](future-tense.md).

## Watch out

The ending נוּ can mean "we did" in the past, or "us" as an object:

| Example | Root | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| חָטָא<b style="color:red">נוּ</b> | חטא | we sinned |
| קִדְּשָׁ<b style="color:red">נוּ</b> | קדש | he sanctified us |
| וְצִוָּ<b style="color:red">נוּ</b> | צוה | and commanded us |

The blessing formula has both object forms:

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁ<b style="color:red">נוּ</b> בְּמִצְוֹתָיו וְצִוָּ<b style="color:red">נוּ</b> | who sanctified us with his commandments and commanded us |
