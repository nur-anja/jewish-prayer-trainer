# Past tense

In the past tense, an ending (not a prefix) shows who did the action. The bare root form means "he did".

| Ending | Who | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| (none) | he | בָּחַר | he chose |
| ־ָה | she | אָמְרָ<b style="color:red">ה</b> | she said |
| ־תִּי | I | אָהַבְ<b style="color:red">תִּי</b> | I loved |
| ־תָּ | you | בָּחַרְ<b style="color:red">תָּ</b> | you chose |
| ־נוּ | we | חָטָא<b style="color:red">נוּ</b> | we sinned |
| ־תֶּם | you (plural) | שְׁמַעְ<b style="color:red">תֶּם</b> | you heard |
| ־וּ | they | שָׁמְר<b style="color:red">וּ</b> | they kept |

## Watch out: "nu"

The "nu" ending can mean "we did" in the past, or "us" as an object:

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| חָטָא<b style="color:red">נוּ</b> | we sinned |
| קִדְּשָׁ<b style="color:red">נוּ</b> | he sanctified us |
| וְצִוָּ<b style="color:red">נוּ</b> | and commanded us |

The blessing formula has both object forms:

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁ<b style="color:red">נוּ</b> בְּמִצְוֹתָיו וְצִוָּ<b style="color:red">נוּ</b> | who sanctified us with his commandments and commanded us |

## "And" that flips to the future

In biblical text, a ו before a past form can turn it into future or command:

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| <b style="color:red">וְ</b>אָהַבְתָּ | and you shall love |
