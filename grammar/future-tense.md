# Future tense (eitan)

In the future tense, a prefix letter shows who does the action. There are four of them, and together they spell the name Eitan:

| Mnemonic | Meaning |
|---|---|
| אֵיתָן | Eitan (also "strong, steadfast") |

| Prefix | Who | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| א | I | <b style="color:red">אֲ</b>בָרֵךְ | I will bless |
| י | he, they | <b style="color:red">יִ</b>מְלֹךְ | he will reign |
| ת | you, she | <b style="color:red">תִּ</b>שְׁמַע | you will hear |
| נ | we | <b style="color:red">נְ</b>בָרֵךְ | we will bless |

"You" plural and "they" also add a ו at the end, for example "they will say":

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| <b style="color:red">יֹ</b>אמְר<b style="color:red">וּ</b> | they will say |

## Future as a wish

In prayers, the future often reads as a wish ("may it…", "let…"):

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| <b style="color:red">יִ</b>תְגַּדַּל | may it be magnified (Kaddish) |
| וְ<b style="color:red">יִ</b>תְקַדַּשׁ | and may it be sanctified |

## "And" that flips to the past

In biblical narrative, a ו before a future form often turns it into past tense. This is common in verses quoted in prayers. The ו usually has a patach, and the next letter a dagesh:

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| <b style="color:red">וַ</b>יֹּאמֶר | and he said |

## Watch out

A word starting with one of these letters isn't always a future verb. The letter may be part of the root, or a [prefix letter](prefix-letters.md) like "and". Check whether a 3-letter root remains after removing it.
