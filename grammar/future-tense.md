# Future tense

In the future tense, a prefix letter shows who does the action. There are four of them, and together they spell the name Eitan:

| Mnemonic | Translation |
|---|---|
| אֵיתָן | Eitan (also "strong, steadfast") |

Each prefix marks one person:

| Prefix | Who | Example | Root | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| אֲ־ | I | <b style="color:red">אֲ</b>בָרֵךְ | ברך | I will bless |
| יִ־ | he, they | <b style="color:red">יִ</b>מְלֹךְ | מלך | he will reign |
| תִּ־ | you, she | <b style="color:red">תִּ</b>שְׁמַע | שמע | you will hear |
| נְ־ | we | <b style="color:red">נְ</b>בָרֵךְ | ברך | we will bless |

The vowel under the prefix varies from verb to verb, but the letter stays the same.

## Plural "you" and "they"

"You" plural and "they" also add a ו at the end:

| Example | Root | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| <b style="color:red">יֹ</b>אמְר<b style="color:red">וּ</b> | אמר | they will say |

## Future as a wish

In prayers, the future often reads as a wish ("may it…", "let…"):

| Example | Root | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| <b style="color:red">יִ</b>תְגַּדַּל | גדל | may it be magnified |
| וְ<b style="color:red">יִ</b>תְקַדַּשׁ | קדש | and may it be sanctified |

Both are the opening words of the Kaddish.

## "And" that flips to the past

In biblical narrative, a ו before a future form often turns it into past tense:

| Example | Root | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| <b style="color:red">וַ</b>יֹּאמֶר | אמר | and he said |

This is common in verses quoted in prayers. The ו usually has a patach, and the next letter a dagesh. The opposite flip, from past to future, is in [past tense](past-tense.md).

## Watch out

A word starting with one of these letters isn't always a future verb, because the letter may be part of the root, or a [prefix letter](prefix-letters.md) like "and". Check whether a 3-letter root remains after removing it.
