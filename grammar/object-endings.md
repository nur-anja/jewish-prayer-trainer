# Object endings

A verb can take an ending for its object ("me", "you", "us", "them") instead of a separate word. They are mostly the same as the [possessive endings](possessive-endings.md) on nouns:

| Ending | Meaning | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| ־נִי | me | עָשַׂ<b style="color:red">נִי</b> | he made me |
| ־ךָ | you (singular) | מְצַוְּ<b style="color:red">ךָ</b> | command you |
| ־וֹ | him, it | לְקַדְּשׁ<b style="color:red">וֹ</b> | to sanctify it |
| ־נוּ | us | יַעֲנֵ<b style="color:red">נוּ</b> | may he answer us |
| ־ם | them | וּכְתַבְתָּ<b style="color:red">ם</b> | and you shall write them |

The vowel before the ending changes from verb to verb, but the ending's letters stay the same. Strip the ending first, then find the tense from what's left: see [past tense](past-tense.md) and [future tense](future-tense.md).

## After an infinitive

After an infinitive, the same endings usually say who does the action, like "my" or "your" on a noun:

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| בְּשִׁבְתְּ<b style="color:red">ךָ</b> | when you sit (in your sitting) |
| וּבְקוּמֶ<b style="color:red">ךָ</b> | and when you rise up (and in your rising) |
| מִבּוֹא<b style="color:red">וֹ</b> | from its coming |

They can also be the object, as on other verbs:

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| לְקַדְּשׁ<b style="color:red">וֹ</b> | to sanctify it |
| לְאַמְּצָ<b style="color:red">ךְ</b> | to strengthen you |

Only the meaning of the sentence tells which one it is.

## Watch out

The ending for "us" looks like the past-tense ending for "we":

| Example | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| חָטָא<b style="color:red">נוּ</b> | "we", past tense | we sinned |
| קִדְּשָׁ<b style="color:red">נוּ</b> | "us", object | he sanctified us |

In poems and at the end of a verse, "you" (singular) is often written with ךְ and a kamatz before it, the form that otherwise means a woman:

| Example | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| יַחֲלִיצָ<b style="color:red">ךְ</b> | kamatz before ךְ | he will deliver you |
| נַעֲרִיצָ<b style="color:red">ךְ</b> | kamatz before ךְ | we will revere you |
