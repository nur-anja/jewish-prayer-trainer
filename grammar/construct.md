# Construct forms ("X of Y")

Hebrew has no separate word for "of". Instead, the first noun changes its form and is placed right before the second. This changed form is called the construct.

| Ending | Meaning | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| ־ֵי | "of" (masculine plural) | אֱלֹהֵ<b style="color:red">י</b> | God of |
| ־ַת | "of" (feminine singular) | תּוֹרַ<b style="color:red">ת</b> | teaching of |

## How the ending changes

The masculine plural ending loses its final ם:

| Normal | Construct | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| אֱלֹהִ<b style="color:red">ים</b> | אֱלֹהֵ<b style="color:red">י</b> | God → God of |
| בָּנִ<b style="color:red">ים</b> | בְּנֵ<b style="color:red">י</b> | sons → sons of |

A feminine ending ה turns into ת:

| Normal | Construct | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| תּוֹרָ<b style="color:red">ה</b> | תּוֹרַ<b style="color:red">ת</b> | teaching → teaching of |
| בְּרָכָ<b style="color:red">ה</b> | בִּרְכַּ<b style="color:red">ת</b> | blessing → blessing of |

Vowels earlier in the word often shorten too, as in the words for sons and blessing above.

Feminine plurals and many masculine singular nouns don't change at all. Only the position (right before another noun) shows it's "of".

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| מִצְוֹת הַתּוֹרָה | commandments of the Torah |
| מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם | king of the world |

## Possessive endings

The construct form is also the base that [possessive endings](possessive-endings.md) attach to. That's why "our God" keeps the י from the plural construct:

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| אֱלֹהֵ<b style="color:red">י</b>נוּ | our God |

## Ending that looks similar

A ת at the end can also be an [abstract-noun ending](nouns.md#the--ut-ending-for-abstract-nouns) rather than "of":

| Example | Translation |
|---|---|
| מַלְכ<b style="color:red">וּת</b> | kingship |
