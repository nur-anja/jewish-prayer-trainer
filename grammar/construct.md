# Construct forms ("X of Y")

Hebrew has no separate word for "of". Instead, the first noun changes its form and is placed right before the second. This changed form is called the construct.

| Ending | Meaning | Example | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| י | "of" (masculine plural) | אלהי | Elohei | God of |
| ת | "of" (feminine singular) | תורת | torat | teaching of |
| ות | "of" (feminine plural) | מצות | mitzvot | commandments of |

## How the ending changes

The masculine plural ending loses its final mem:

| Normal | Construct | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| אלהים | אלהי | Elohim → Elohei | God → God of |
| בנים | בני | banim → benei | sons → sons of |

A feminine ending he turns into tav:

| Normal | Construct | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| תורה | תורת | torah → torat | teaching → teaching of |
| ברכה | ברכת | berakhah → birkat | blessing → blessing of |

The feminine plural ending looks the same either way. Only the vowels change, if at all.

## Watch out

Many masculine singular nouns look the same in the construct. Only the position (right before another noun) shows it's "of".

| Example | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| מלך העולם | melekh ha-olam | king of the world |

The construct form is also the base that [possessive endings](possessive-endings.md) attach to. That's why "our God" keeps the yod from the plural construct:

| Example | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| אלהינו | Eloheinu | our God |

## Ending that looks similar

A tav at the end can also be an abstract-noun ending rather than "of":

| Example | Transliteration | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| מלכות | malkhut | kingship |
