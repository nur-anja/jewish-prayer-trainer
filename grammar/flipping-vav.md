# Flipping vav

In biblical Hebrew, a ו in front of a verb can flip its tense: a future form becomes past, and a past form becomes future or a command. It is written with the same letter as the [prefix letter](prefix-letters.md) for "and", and it still means "and":

| Prefix | Meaning | Example | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| וַ־ | and (future form read as past) | <b style="color:red">וַ</b>יֹּאמֶר | and he said |
| וְ־ | and (past form read as future) | <b style="color:red">וְ</b>אָהַבְתָּ | and you shall love |

Flipping vavs are common in the Torah verses quoted in prayers, and rare in prayers written later.

## On a future form

Before a [future](future-tense.md) form, the flipping ו has a patach, and the tense prefix right after it has a dagesh:

| Prefix | Example | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| וַ־ | <b style="color:red">וַ</b>יַּעַשׂ | dagesh in the י | and he made |
| וַ־ | <b style="color:red">וַ</b>נִּצְעַק | dagesh in the נ | and we cried out |
| וָ־ | <b style="color:red">וָ</b>אֹמַר | kamatz, no dagesh | and I said |

The א of "I" can't take a dagesh, so the ו has a kamatz there instead of a patach.

## On a past form

Before a [past](past-tense.md) form, the flipping ו looks exactly like the plain "and", so only the context tells them apart:

| Prefix | Example | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| וְ־ | <b style="color:red">וְ</b>אָהַבְתָּ | follows a command | and you shall love |
| וּ־ | <b style="color:red">וּ</b>כְתַבְתָּם | shuruk before a sheva | and you shall write them |

The verb continues a command or a future event before it, so it is read as future or as a command. In carefully pointed texts the stress often moves to the last syllable, but most prayer books don't mark stress.

## Telling it apart from plain "and"

A plain "and" keeps the tense of the verb after it. Before a future form, it has a sheva (or a shuruk before a sheva or before ב, מ, פ) and no dagesh in the tense prefix after it:

| Flipping | Plain | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| <b style="color:red">וַ</b>יִּקָּרֵא | <b style="color:red">וְ</b>יִקָּרֵא | and it was called → and may it be called |
| <b style="color:red">וְ</b>אָהַבְתָּ | <b style="color:red">וְ</b>אָהַבְתָּ | and you shall love → and you loved |

So check the verb after the ו:

1. **Future form:** a patach or kamatz under the ו, and a dagesh in the tense prefix, means it flips: read it as past. A sheva or shuruk means plain "and": read it as future, or as a wish in a prayer.
2. **Past form:** the vowels don't help. If it continues a command or a promise, it flips: read it as future. If it continues a past event, it is plain "and": read it as past.

## Watch out

A patach under the ו, or a dagesh after it, doesn't always mean a flip, and a flip doesn't always show a dagesh:

| Example | Note | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| <b style="color:red">וַ</b>אֲנִי | plain "and": patach only because of the hataf patach after it | and I |
| <b style="color:red">וַ</b>יְהִי | flips, but the י has no dagesh because it has a sheva | and it was |
| <b style="color:red">וְ</b>יִקָּרֵא | plain "and": the dagesh is in the ק, a root letter, not in the tense prefix | and may it be called |
| <b style="color:red">וְ</b>צִוָּנוּ | plain "and" before a past form: the blessing tells what God did | and commanded us |
